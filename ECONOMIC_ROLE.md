# IPX Economic Role

**Category:** Nearly sellable → direct-revenue infrastructure/business

## Economic objective
Turn IPX into a commercially credible product whose engineering roadmap is controlled by buyer value, pricing power, and transaction readiness rather than feature accumulation.

## Target customer
Organizations and teams that need the capabilities represented by IPX and are willing to pay for a differentiated, controlled experience rather than assemble fragmented alternatives.

## Problem worth paying for
Deliver a coherent product experience with strong technical execution, clear ownership of the user journey, and commercial capabilities that can be sold at pricing comparable to the value delivered by incumbent alternatives.

## Current sellable capability
Existing IPX product architecture and implemented product surface, with the commercial direction centered on selling IPX's own version of the capabilities it provides rather than acting as a thin reseller.

## Distribution
Direct sales, founder-led enterprise outreach, product-led demonstrations, technical proof, partnerships, and customer referrals.

## Monetization
Direct product revenue, enterprise contracts, usage-based or packaged pricing where appropriate, and transaction/payment collection through Stripe or another payment rail while IPX retains control of its own UI, invoices, product packaging, and customer experience.

## Moat
Integrated product experience, proprietary architecture and workflow, cross-portfolio technology leverage, speed of iteration, and control over the commercial surface rather than dependence on a third-party front end.

## Immediate economic blockers
Precise offer packaging, buyer segmentation, pricing architecture, payment/invoice completion, deployable customer path, contract/support expectations, and proof that the product is materially better for its intended buyer.

## Next highest-value action
Finish the minimum commercial path that allows a real buyer to understand the offer, agree to terms, pay, receive access, and successfully use the product. Any engineering work that does not improve one of those steps should be deprioritized unless it protects core IP or closes a material reliability/security risk.

## Not now
Do not add arbitrary subscription tiers, unrelated payment products, or engineering work disconnected from the actual IPX offer and buyer.

## Evidence required
Qualified buyer interest, paid transactions/contracts, successful customer onboarding, usage/retention, margin, and technical proof tied to buyer decisions.
