# IPX Commercial Completion Model

IPX is a private modern patent office. It sells IPX services directly; it does not treat government fees as pass-through revenue unless a separately disclosed government-filing workflow is explicitly purchased.

## Base price-parity doctrine

For an IPX service that is intentionally equivalent to a service offered by the USPTO, the IPX base price targets the currently published USPTO price for the matched service and entity tier. The public fee is a competitive reference, not a remittance. Quotes must record the matched service code, authority, source URL, source revision, effective date, verification timestamp, and IPX quote hash.

The base IPX service should provide more value than the matched legacy transaction wherever technically and legally possible, including provenance, lifecycle history, evidence integrity, private records, exportability, auditability, status continuity, and integrated commercialization workflows.

Government filing, statutory rights, official examination, or government-issued registration are separate where law requires them.

## Revenue surfaces

IPX should not cap monetization at office-equivalent transaction fees. Commercially justified revenue surfaces include:

- office-equivalent private filing, examination, search, record, certificate, renewal, maintenance, assignment, and portfolio services;
- IPX Watchdog recurring monitoring subscriptions;
- encrypted vault storage and long-term preservation tiers;
- portfolio subscriptions and organization seats;
- priority processing and service-level commitments where lawful;
- professional-supported workflows and expert review;
- API usage, developer plans, enterprise integrations, and white-label infrastructure;
- diligence rooms and transaction-readiness packages;
- licensing, assignment, transfer, and marketplace transaction fees;
- commercialization and capital-readiness services;
- international/jurisdiction orchestration and deadline management;
- custom monitoring sources, custom rules, webhook/API alert delivery, and enterprise Watchdog policies;
- evidence export, archival preservation, escrow-like continuity services, and succession/legacy services;
- analytics and portfolio intelligence derived from the customer's own authorized portfolio data;
- institutional deployments for law firms, universities, research organizations, accelerators, enterprises, and professional-service networks.

## Margin doctrine

Every service must record direct variable cost, provider cost, labor/professional cost, storage cost, payment-processing cost, support cost, acquisition cost, and expected loss/fraud cost. Price parity is not a license to operate at an unknown margin.

High-margin software revenue should subsidize access improvements without concealing professional or government costs. Margin should be measured by service code and cohort. A service that cannot reach an acceptable contribution margin after a defined learning period must be redesigned, automated, repriced where the parity doctrine does not apply, bundled differently, or removed.

## Commercial completion gate

A revenue surface is commercially complete only when it has:

1. an implemented production workflow;
2. authentication, authorization, tenant isolation, auditability, and data controls;
3. deterministic pricing and quote provenance;
4. payment and entitlement enforcement;
5. cancellation/refund/dispute handling where applicable;
6. idempotency and duplicate-charge protection;
7. support and operational ownership;
8. observability and incident response;
9. documented customer terms and product/legal boundaries;
10. measured unit economics;
11. production deployment and recovery procedures;
12. evidence that a real customer can complete the promised workflow without mocks or manual hidden steps.

## Watchdog economics

Watchdog is recurring monitoring infrastructure, not a one-time search. Tiers may differ by asset count, scan cadence, source coverage, alert channels, evidence preservation, analyst escalation, custom rules, API access, and white-label support. Automated findings are possible misuse/infringement signals, not legal determinations.

## Expansion rule

New monetization pathways are allowed whenever they create genuine customer value, preserve trust, comply with applicable law, and do not compromise evidentiary neutrality. IPX should prefer compounding recurring and transaction revenue over artificial product limitations.
