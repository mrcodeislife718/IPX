# IPX

**Intellectual Property Trust Infrastructure**

IPX is a commercial evidence, provenance, verification, protection, preservation, and economic infrastructure platform for intellectual property.

It enables inventors, creators, software developers, researchers, businesses, attorneys, and intellectual-property owners to create durable evidence records, protect confidential materials, document contribution and provenance, issue independently verifiable certificates, manage rights over time, monitor risk, and move protected work toward licensing and commercial use.

IPX is not merely a certificate generator, timestamping utility, encrypted file vault, portfolio tracker, marketplace, or blockchain application. It integrates those functions into one governed intellectual-property lifecycle.

> **Independent-platform disclosure:** IPX is a private evidence and provenance platform. An IPX record does not itself create a patent, copyright registration, trademark registration, government filing, legal ownership determination, or court judgment.

Documents authorship, inventorship, human contribution, AI involvement, software provenance, research lineage, media origin, and organizational participation.

### IPX Legacy™

Supports long-term preservation, succession, beneficiary planning, business continuity, key replacement, provider migration, and durable verification beyond account cancellation or temporary platform unavailability.

### IPX Guard™

Monitors protected assets, records potential conflicts or misuse, preserves response evidence, manages review workflows, and supports escalation without making unsupported legal determinations.

### IPX Portfolio™

Organizes intellectual-property assets, evidence status, ownership claims, contributors, agreements, commercial readiness, deadlines, preservation requirements, and portfolio-level reporting.

## End-to-end evidence lifecycle

```text
Account and identity assurance
    -> IP asset record
    -> confidential evidence upload
    -> canonicalization
    -> cryptographic commitment
    -> trusted timestamp
    -> claimant and contributor signatures
    -> optional external anchoring
    -> transparency receipt
    -> CertID issuance
    -> deterministic certificate rendering
    -> public/private verification
    -> preservation, amendment, transfer, or revocation
```

Every consequential stage binds the exact canonical payload to the actor, authority, identity-assurance level, policy version, cryptographic algorithms, key version, trusted time, verification result, and lifecycle status.

## Evidence and certificate system

IPX records can include:

- unique CertID and short verification code;
- canonical public commitment;
- protected source-hash reference;
- claimant, organization, contributor, author, and inventor records;
- trusted timestamp and provider information;
- claimant, contributor, and issuer signatures;
- external anchor and transparency receipts;
- chain-of-custody history;
- verification status;
- governing evidence-policy version;
- key fingerprint and algorithm identifiers;
- amendment, correction, supersession, challenge, dispute, and revocation history;
- machine-readable evidence manifest;
- independently checkable verification instructions.

Deterministic rendering supports controlled outputs such as:

- signed PDF;
- archival PDF/A;
- canonical HTML and SVG;
- PNG presentation copy;
- machine-readable JSON;
- public, private, redacted, monochrome, and confidential variants;
- render manifest and render hash.

Visual presentation is informative; cryptographic verification remains authoritative.

## Privacy architecture

IPX separates evidence integrity from personal and confidential information.

### Immutable evidence layer

- blinded commitments;
- timestamp tokens;
- signatures;
- transparency receipts;
- policy identifiers;
- non-sensitive status statements.

### Mutable privacy layer

- display names;
- contact information;
- organization metadata;
- identity-provider references;
- public descriptions;
- access permissions.

### Encrypted confidential layer

- evidence files;
- agreements;
- private descriptions;
- identity-supporting documents;
- source materials;
- private salts and disclosure keys.

The architecture supports selective disclosure, crypto-shredding, key destruction, legal holds, redacted exports, tombstone records, regional retention, and minimum necessary public metadata.
